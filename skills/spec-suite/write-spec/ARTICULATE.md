# Articulate

Users often know more than they have said, and that knowledge is easiest to draw out while its branch is still live. Articulation asks the branch's next question through a named method, so the user supplies the substance and the method supplies the angle.

## When

Reach for a method on a one-way door, when the user plainly knows more than they have said:

- a thin or hedged answer;
- a recommendation accepted without engaging its trade-off;
- a branch settled on an assumption you supplied rather than one the user stated.

A crisp answer that carries the user's own decision is theirs to make: accept it and move on. Use a method at most once per branch unless the user engages with it.

## How

Ask one question through one method, as a numbered question in the round, naming the method in passing ("Pre-mortem: it's six months out and..."). When the answer lives only in the user's head, use the _What only the user knows_ marker in place of a recommendation. Fold the answer into the tree and state the change in one line.

The user may name any method. Run it if you know it; otherwise ask what they mean.

## Methods

Pick by the weakness in front of you:

- **Socratic Questioning**: a hedged or vague answer.
- **5 Whys Deep Dive**: a solution named before its problem.
- **Reframe the Question**: an answer to a different question than the one that matters.
- **Pre-mortem Analysis**: a success signal just settled; its answer often becomes the counter-signal.
- **Assumption Audit**: a choice resting on something the user never stated.
- **First Principles Analysis**: "because that's how it's done".
- **Steelmanning**: a recommendation accepted without its alternative being heard.
- **Challenge from Critical Perspective**: a synthesis accepted without engaging its trade-offs.
- **Abstraction Laddering**: a requirement too abstract to test, or too concrete to explain.
- **Stakeholder Lens Rotation**: requirements seen from a single actor's viewpoint.
- **Second-Order Thinking**: a decision whose knock-on effects nobody has followed.
- **Subtraction**: scope larger than the problem needs.
- **Failure Mode Analysis**: an architecture decision with no failure behaviour.
- **Boundary & Edge Case Sweep**: inputs or limits that no story covers.
